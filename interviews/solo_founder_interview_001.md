# Interview Transcript: Solo Founder - Sarah Chen

**Date:** 2024-01-15  
**Duration:** 45 minutes  
**Interviewer:** Product Research Team  
**Participant:** Sarah Chen, Founder of TaskFlow (Productivity SaaS)  
**Persona:** solo_founder

---

## Background

Sarah is building TaskFlow, a productivity management tool for small teams. She's been working solo for 8 months and just launched her MVP. She's bootstrapping with $15K personal savings.

---

## Key Quotes & Pain Points

### Time Pressure & Resource Constraints

**Interviewer:** "What's your biggest challenge right now in product development?"

**Sarah:** _"Honestly, it's the constant context switching. I'll spend 3 hours coding a feature, then realize I have no idea if users actually want it. Like last month, I built this elaborate tagging system - took me two weeks - and when I showed it to my beta users, they were confused and never used it. **Two weeks of work, completely wasted.** I can't afford to do that again."_

**Sarah:** _"Every hour I spend building the wrong thing is an hour I'm not spending on what could actually drive revenue. When you're bootstrapped, you literally don't have time for mistakes."_

### Feature Validation Struggles

**Interviewer:** "How do you currently decide what features to build?"

**Sarah:** _"That's the problem - I don't have a good system. Right now I just... guess? I read through user feedback emails, look at support tickets, maybe post in a Slack channel. But it's so scattered. **I wish I had some way to score ideas against real user evidence before I start coding.**"_

**Sarah:** _"I see other founders talking about 'customer development' and 'validation frameworks,' but when you're building alone, who has time for that? I need something that just... works. **Minimal overhead, maximum insight.**"_

### Tool Integration Frustrations

**Interviewer:** "What tools do you currently use for planning and development?"

**Sarah:** _"I basically live in VS Code and Linear. Sometimes Notion for docs. The problem is everything is in different places. User feedback is in email, feature ideas are in Linear, actual evidence is... nowhere? **I need something that works inside the tools I already use**, not another dashboard I have to remember to check."_

### Evidence vs Intuition

**Sarah:** _"I'm a developer, not a PM. My instinct is to build what seems technically interesting, but that's exactly the wrong approach. **I need something to force me to prove user demand before I write a single line of code.** Like, literally block me from starting until I have evidence."_

**Interviewer:** "What would that look like?"

**Sarah:** _"Maybe a score? Like, 'This feature has 20% evidence coverage' vs '80% evidence coverage.' **If it's below some threshold, red flag - don't build it yet.** Simple, visual, integrated into my workflow."_

### Success Metrics

**Sarah:** _"I'll know this is working when I stop building features nobody uses. Right now, I'd guess 60% of what I build gets ignored. **If I could cut that to 20%, I'd save probably 1-2 sprints per month.** That's huge when you're moving fast."_

---

## Key Insights for Evidence Engine

1. **Validation before coding:** Strong need for evidence gates before development starts
2. **Time sensitivity:** Every wasted sprint is critical for bootstrapped founders
3. **Tool integration:** Must work within existing development workflow (VS Code, Linear)
4. **Scoring system:** Simple, visual evidence scores (0-100) for quick decision making
5. **Minimal overhead:** Cannot add complex processes to already constrained workflow
